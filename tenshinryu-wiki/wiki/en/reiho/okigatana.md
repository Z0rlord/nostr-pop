# Okigatana 置刀 — Placing the Sword

Edo-period samurai removed the long sword indoors; they did not sit with it at the waist. **In Tenshinryu training, never sit in Ōgiza wearing both swords.** (A rare exception: teaching without enough wakizashi, the long sword may substitute for one.) Koza, yagura, orishiki and sonkyo may be taken while wearing the long sword. Kuwami notes that schools practicing seated techniques in seiza wearing only the long sword preserve their own meaningful teachings and are not to be disparaged; equally, Tenshinryu's own convention, part of its identity, must be kept strictly (Kuwami 2026, "On Okigatana").

Once seated, the long sword (honzashi 本指) is placed beside or behind you: **okigatana**, also **yasumedachi 休太刀**. It is not a sword abandoned on the floor; it stays as close as readiness requires, and the placement trains you never to leave it behind in an emergency or pick up another's sword by mistake.

Common points for all placements: hilt forward, tip backward, cutting-edge side toward your body, and the line from tsukagashira 柄頭 to kojiri 小尻 **parallel to your facing direction** (because the blade curves, aligning only the hilt angles the scabbard tip outward). At [[concepts/maai|gyō-no-ma]] or wider, the tsuba aligns with the front of the knees; at shin-no-ma, move the sword back so the **pommel** aligns with the knees, keeping the hilt from crossing the boundary between you and the other person.

## 1. In no Okigatana 陰 — on the right

**The form for facing a superior.** Right-handedness was once enforced, and the draw was a right-hand draw from the left hip; a sword on the right forces a left-hand take-up or a hand transfer, declaring no intent to use it. Leave **two to three fist-widths** between sword and body, signaling you do not mean to take it up.

## 2. Yō no Okigatana 陽 — on the left

For equals, juniors, or close company, and in tense situations to show you remain on guard: from the left, the left hand takes the scabbard and the right grips the hilt for an immediate draw. Leave **one fist-width** for instant take-up.

## 3. An no Okigatana 暗 — behind

**The most respectful arrangement when you keep the sword** rather than entrusting it, and the option when either side is obstructed. Normally pommel toward In (right), tip toward Yō (left), edge toward the body, the sword crosswise and parallel to the back, its midpoint aligned with your center; some techniques angle it slightly. At rank-conscious gatherings, if the kamiza is to one side, place the sword so the **tip does not point toward it**.

When a household holds your sword and places it behind you, the edge may face away and the hilt direction is unknown: discreetly check how it lies before you need it.
