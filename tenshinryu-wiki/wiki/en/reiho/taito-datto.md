# Taitō 帯刀 and Dattō 脱刀

Taitō is inserting a sword, scabbard and all, into the sash (kakuobi 角帯); dattō is removing it from the sash. Dattō is **not** drawing the blade. In Tenshinryu the wakizashi (kodachi 小太刀) is always worn in training, the long sword never alone, matching historical daily practice. **Wakizashi on first, long sword off first.** In training and demonstration the thumb restrains the tsuba during both actions; in samurai daily life the scabbard was held slightly beyond the kurikata toward the kojiri (Kuwami 2026, "On Taitō and Dattō").

Before sitting in Ōgiza, **remove the long sword first**; never sit in Ōgiza wearing both swords (see [[reiho/okigatana]]). Koza, yagura, orishiki, sonkyo may be taken while wearing it.

## The basics of taitō

- Right hand holds the sword, thumb on the guard (inner side, not over the edge); the left hand opens a space between the wraps of the kakuobi; insert the kojiri with the sword **near horizontal**. Beginners hold it too vertical, which both jams the insertion and slows any response.
- **Keep looking forward.** If you lose the opening, glance down once, hold it with the left hand, face forward again, then insert.
- Samurai put swords on and off constantly; these rules keep the movement from creating openings.

**Wakizashi 脇指:** between the **first and second wraps** of the kakuobi, kojiri passed over the hakama cords without letting the scabbard end hang low. Right after insertion, press the pommel end firmly downward to bring it near horizontal, keeping its hilt clear of the long sword. Wear it directed straight across the body; inserted from the front, its hilt fouls your arm and the long sword's hilt.

**Honzashi 本指 (本差):** the long sword; note Tenshinryu writes the short sword 脇指 against the general 脇差. Insert between the **second and third wraps**, a sash layer separating the scabbards, honzashi above wakizashi; from above the two swords cross in an X.

## Four ways of wearing the honzashi

1. **Kannuki-zashi 閂指:** near horizontal like a gate bar (kannuki), hilt slightly raised. Generally the pommel points straight forward; **Tenshinryu positions the guard at the body's center**. The school's basic wear, best suited to its drawing principles.
2. **Sekirei-zashi 鶺鴒指:** scabbard end slightly lowered, like a wagtail's (sekirei) tail. Generally the pommel points about forward; in Tenshinryu it points diagonally right, at the center of the body. The general form was popular in the Edo period for its balance.
3. **Otoshi-zashi 落指:** more upright at the left hip, kojiri dropped ("otosu", to lower), pommel up. For crowds and confined spaces, when running, and with long weapons or kusarigama 鎖鎌.
4. **Tenjin-zashi 天神指:** cutting edge **down**, both ends curving up like the kasagi 笠木 of a torii 鳥居 (origin of the name unverified). Used with tachi mountings (worn edge-down by design), in armor, and in vigorous movement or on horseback against sayabashiri.

## Dattō

- Remove the long sword first, then the wakizashi. Normally the right hand draws the sword out while the **left hand in hirade stays engaged at the left hip** where the scabbard passes the sash; a left hand hanging idle is **asobite 遊び手**, a hand with no assigned role. Do not use both hands on the sword.
- Gaze forward, sword near horizontal.
- From sekirei-zashi or otoshi-zashi: the left hand first moves the sword slightly forward, then the right takes it.
- Among close company or facing a junior, the sword may be removed with the **left hand**, pommel kept slightly raised rather than horizontal.
