# Yakujō 約定 (Kinchō 金打)

Yakujō means a promise or agreement. In Tenshinryu it names the ceremonial **kinchō** performed at the beginning and end of every training session (Kuwami 2026, "On Yakujō").

## Background

Kinchō, striking metal against metal for its sound, was widespread in the Edo period as the sign of an oath; the word itself came to mean a promise. Samurai rang tsuba against koiguchi, struck sword guards together, or tapped a kozuka 小柄 against the blade near the guard; Buddhist monks used small gongs, women struck mirrors. Metal's resonance was taken as a sign of purity, and because metal was precious the custom extended to striking personally valued objects.

In Tenshinryu, training itself was official duty for a samurai of the Shirin-dan 士林団. The yakujō pledges: to devote oneself to training, to value and internalize the teachings received, and **not to disclose secret teachings**.

## Procedure

1. The kumigashira 組頭 (group leader) sits in [[reiho/zaho-seated-postures|Koza]]; the others sit in Ōgiza.
2. All bow together with [[reiho/bows|shurei]].
3. The leader rises from Koza onto both knees; the others raise the right knee ([[reiho/zaho-seated-postures|Orishiki]]).
4. With the left thumb, [[concepts/koiguchi|release the blade slightly at the koiguchi]]; grasp the tsukagashira 柄頭 with the right hand in a reverse grip.
5. At the leader's call of **"Kinchō!"**, all bring tsuba to koiguchi at once, ringing the sound.
6. Slide the right hand along the hilt toward the tsuba as if stroking it, lower the hand, and release the sword with the left.
