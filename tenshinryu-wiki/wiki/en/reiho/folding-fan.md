# The Folding Fan (Sensu 扇子 / Ōgi 扇)

Samurai carried a folding fan year-round: for cooling, for waving a breeze, for hiding the mouth while speaking, for pointing directions, as a substitute tray when presenting items, and as a symbolic boundary in greetings. In formal settings they used the **kawahori-sensu 蝙蝠扇子**, a white "bat fan" of one shaku two sun (about 36 cm), matching the military fan (gunsen 軍扇); only samurai could carry it, making it a status symbol (Kuwami 2026, "About the Folding Fan").

## Carrying it in Tenshinryu

Reflecting that heritage, and because many techniques use the fan, **the fan is worn at the right waist in training as well as in demonstration (embu 演武)**, not only on formal occasions. (The sword occupies the left hip; the left-hip fan of modern wedding dress is a sword substitute.) Easiest timing: insert it after putting on the wakizashi.

- Hold the fan's middle with the right hand, **pivot (kaname 要) facing left**, and insert it deeply at an angle between the upper garment and the kakuobi 角帯.
- Too shallow and it falls out; vertical and it fouls movement.
- For demonstration use the kawahori-sensu; for training a dance fan (**mai-sen / maiōgi 舞扇**, about 27-28.5 cm) substitutes, as smaller everyday fans did historically.
- **Sensu-geiko 扇子稽古:** simplified paired training with the fan standing in for a sword, for striking and receiving practice and solo review.

## Jisen, Chisen, Kaisen, Hasen

| Term | Japanese | Meaning |
|------|----------|---------|
| Jisen | 持扇 | holding the fan |
| Chisen | 置扇 | placing it down |
| Kaisen | 懐扇 | putting it away inside the kimono front |
| Hasen | 把扇 | picking it up |

After sitting, remove the fan from the waist and hold it in the **left hand** (jisen). In formal ceremony, hold it with **both hands on the thighs**. To free the hands: place it beside the right knee (chisen), tuck it into the kimono front (kaisen), or back into the sash.

## Placing the fan for a seated bow

For formal respects to a superior, the fan may be placed **in front of the knees** before a [[reiho/bows|zarei]]: it draws a boundary between self and other, and removing it substitutes for removing the wakizashi, both expressions of respect.

1. After sitting, take the fan from the waist with the right hand.
2. Place it before the knees, **pivot pointing right, front side (omote 表) up**.
3. Bow. Then quietly pick it up with the right hand and return it to the sash.

Not used for abbreviated bows (ryakurei 略礼).
