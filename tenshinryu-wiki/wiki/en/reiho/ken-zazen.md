# Ken-zazen 剣坐禅 — Sword Zazen

At Kōgan 光願, daily training opened with warm-ups, then the low windows (jimado 地窓) of the hall were opened and practitioners sat in a row before them or along the veranda, performing zazen in the morning sun. The ritual clears everyday distraction, and cultivates **fudōshin 不動心** by holding the awareness of jōzai-senjō 常在戦場, always on the battlefield, never apart from death (Kuwami 2026, "On Ken-zazen").

Where Zen uses kekkafuza 結跏趺坐 (full lotus), Tenshinryu sits in **Koza 胡坐**, swords never out of reach: hence "sword zazen".

## Sword arrangements (three options)

1. Keep both swords at the waist.
2. Remove only the long sword; place it right, left or rear ([[concepts/spatial-terminology|In 陰, Yō 陽, or An 暗]]).
3. Remove both: long sword placed right, left or rear; short sword crosswise beneath both thighs.

## Form

- Join the palms before the chest, then settle the hands together against the lower abdomen at the **seika tanden 臍下丹田**, left hand under, right over, thumbs leaving a paper-thickness gap that must not close. (Zen's hokkai jōin 法界定印; with a sword worn, the hands rest beneath its hilt.)
- Back straight, chin drawn back as a whole-head movement, not a nod.
- Eyes half-open (**hangan 半眼**), gaze lowered along the nose line toward the thumb gap, which you will not actually see.
- Breathe in slowly through the nose, out even more slowly through the mouth.
- **Three to four minutes** is the guideline; no strict duration. Afterward, perform [[reiho/yakujo-kincho|kinchō]] and begin training.

Ken-zazen is still practiced before training today; if you arrive late, do your own three to four minutes before joining.
