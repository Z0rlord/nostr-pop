# Kikyo 起居 and Chakuza 着座 — Rising and Sitting

In samurai formal conduct (buke-reihō 武家礼法), even standing up and sitting down are governed in detail: the etiquette shows non-hostility and grace, prevents openings (suki 隙), and builds body control (Kuwami 2026, "On Kikyo").

**Two standing rules:**

1. **Never place hands on the floor**, sitting or rising. Free hands signal readiness and force weight control, which produces calm movement.
2. **Gaze toward the other person's knees.** An unfixed gaze makes the whole posture look unsettled.

## Hakama-sabaki 袴捌き

When sitting (chakuza), the hakama hem catches on heels or gets stepped on; hakama-sabaki prevents that in advance and tidies the garment once seated. If both hands are free, use both; with one hand occupied (sagetō), work one-handed. Knees go down **one at a time**; the simplified both-knees method creates openings and is not used. Beginners may sweep firmly for neatness; in real practice, move quietly enough not to raise dust.

### Unarmed (mute 無手), into Ōgiza — four methods

The names are provisional and need not be memorized. All four end the same way: left knee down slightly opened left, right knee opened nearly sideways then turned forward into yagura, hems drawn out, insteps down, hips lowered into [[reiho/zaho-seated-postures|Ōgiza]].

| Method | Japanese | Idea |
|--------|----------|------|
| Maehiza-suri | 前膝擦り | Palms stroke from groin to below the knees, hands lowering in a V (fingertips never pointing straight down) |
| Hizaura-suri | 膝裏擦り | Tegatana hands lift the hems from behind the knees (leaving space behind the knee), then move to the front before bending |
| Tsumami-age | 摘み上げ | Pinch and slightly lift the hakama near the bottom of the side opening (aibiki-domari 相引き止まり) |
| Matawari | 股割り | Hands drop between the thighs from beneath the groin and sweep the cloth aside |

### In Sagetō 提刀 — two one-handed methods

Only for when the sword is carried in the right hand; never use these unarmed. Sequence as above, then lay the sword to the right, pinch the hems with both hands, lift the hips slightly and draw the hems out.

- **Kata-suso Barai 片裾払い** (single-hem sweep): for beginners; sweep the left hem, then the right, one at a time.
- **Ryo-suso Barai 両裾払い** (double-hem sweep): sweep left then immediately right in one quick action, turning the wrist over between.

### Handling the hems once seated

Either spread the hems into a neat triangle, or, when lateral space is tight or swords are placed beside you, fold the spread hems in tidily so they cannot slip under or cover a sword. Never leave hems crumpled.

## Sitting in Koza

Pinch the hakama slightly up near the aibiki-domari. Draw the left foot behind the right to cross the shins, bend the knees, set the left foot on its toes, place the left knee down, then lower the instep and hips into Koza. In tabi, adjust so no shin shows between hakama and tabi. In sagetō, the sword hand pinches the hakama with its fingers while still holding the sword; set the sword down after sitting.

## Standing up

1. Set both toes up into **kiza 跪坐** first; do not rise from flat insteps.
2. At **shin-no-ma** (close distance): rise straight up with both feet together so as not to close the gap. This is **chochin-dachi 提灯立**, after a paper lantern's extension.
3. At **gyō-no-ma 行の間 or wider**: from kiza, advance the right foot only slightly into a half-kneel (kata-hiza-dachi); the right heel should not pass the left knee. Rise quietly, then draw the right foot back level with the left. Both the small step and the withdrawal preserve [[concepts/maai|maai]].
4. From **okigatana 置刀**: pick up the sword to the hip first, then kiza, then as above.

From Koza: advance the front (upper) leg slightly, shift weight forward, set the rear toes up into a half-kneel, rise straight, draw the front foot back. With okigatana, retrieve the sword first.
