The Later Tenshinryu and the Future Tenshinryu
Afterward, Tenshinryu began to expand its presence, although initially only within the
Kanto region. With the creation of a website, the uploading of videos, and
promotional activities through various social media platforms, the number of disciples
grew significantly compared to earlier years.
In 2015, as public recognition of Tenshinryu increased, external advisors pointed out
the growing risk of trademark infringement. In response, Japanese domestic
trademark rights were secured under the name Edo Yagyu Tenshinryu Hyoho.
In 2016, to strengthen social credibility, manage funds and assets more effectively,
and ensure smooth organizational operations, the NPO Tenshinryu Hyoho was
established.
In 2019, the first year of the Reiwa era, Tenshinryu Online was launched, allowing
members from across Japan and abroad to study Tenshinryu remotely via the internet.
In the same year, the NPO transitioned into a new legal structure with the
establishment of the General Incorporated Association Tenshinryu Hyoho.
Tenshinryu's social media accounts have risen to near the top ranks among those of
traditional martial arts (koryu) styles, and the style has gained remarkable recognition
both in Japan and internationally.
However, this success also marked the beginning of intense slander and interference
from practitioners and instructors of other koryu and modern budo disciplines.
Some individuals mistakenly perceived Tenshinryu as a newly created style due to its
long period of obscurity, despite Tenshin Sensei's many years of activity. Others were
unable to tolerate its rising popularity or the seemingly unconventional nature of its
promotional methods.
Additionally, many lacked a proper understanding of Edo-period martial arts
traditions. With their biased interpretations, they misunderstood Tenshinryu's
techniques and publicly ridiculed them out of ignorance.
Above all, jealousy over Tenshinryu's growing prominence became a major driver of
slander.
Even now, such acts of interference continue to cause significant harm to Tenshinryu.



Although Tenshinryu's recognition expanded and the number of students increased,
numerous challenges remained, and the situation was far from stable. Tenshin Sensei
struggled to understand these issues and consistently prioritized his own self-centered
desires. When things did not go exactly as he wished, he would become enraged,
subjecting me to harsh criticism and abusive language on a daily basis—whether in
person, over the phone, or even by fax.
Furthermore, he frequently spoke ill of me and the Tenshinryu students to others,
including unrelated individuals, fellow students, and associates.
Despite being in a position where he should have acted in Tenshinryu's best interest,
Tenshin Sensei instead created constant obstacles.
To make matters worse, several students repeatedly caused serious problems, and I
found myself constantly occupied with managing these incidents.
Amidst all of this, beginning around 2017, I began to experience facial paralysis from
overwork and excessive stress, along with frequent headaches that seemed to be early
signs of a potential stroke.
While Tenshin Sensei is still alive and could appoint the next Shike as a guardian in
the event of my passing, ensuring the legitimacy and continuity of the lineage requires
designating a successor in advance—even if it may seem premature. In preparation
for any unforeseen circumstances, I formally passed on the title of 11th Shike (師家,
head of the style) to Ide Ryusetsu Sensei in 2019.

This decision may indeed have been early, but in truth, my own succession as the 10th
Shike was also premature at the time.
People can always grow and mature. While our traditions are immutable in essence,
they are not indestructible. Without the formal recognition of a successor, the very
existence of the lineage could be threatened.

Succession Ceremony of the 11th Shike, Reiwa 1 (2019)

As you know, shortly afterward, the world was struck by the COVID-19 pandemic,
making it difficult for Tenshinryu to even maintain regular training. For nearly a year,
we were unable to access our usual practice spaces. However, martial arts—born from
life-and-death struggles—exist to teach us how to live and how to help others live,
even in times of unprecedented crisis. This was truly a moment when the true value of
the Katsujinken (活人剣, the life-giving sword) was put to the test.
We focused on preserving and transmitting our traditions through a variety of efforts,
including Tenshinryu Online, which we had launched just before the pandemic, and
by developing innovative new training methods. Perhaps the most significant
achievement was the digital recording and sharing of training sessions at the
headquarters, presented as Tenshin Sensei's Direct Instruction Seminars.
