Tenshinryu Hyoho System
Japanese martial arts reached their peak during the Edo period. The samurai governed
in times of peace, but when incidents occurred or wars broke out, they risked their
lives fighting for their lord, their clan, or their own honor. Therefore, even during
peacetime, samurai never forgot the mindset of always being on the battlefield. To
cultivate their mind, technique, and body, martial arts were promoted and rigorously
practiced. It is said that during the Edo period, there were several hundred—or by
some accounts, over a thousand—different martial arts styles.
In earlier times, many martial arts styles were comprehensive systems that included
techniques spanning various categories of weapons. However, as time passed, these
styles became more specialized and fragmented, resulting in the loss of their overall
versatility. Nevertheless, because samurai needed to be prepared for any situation,
they trained in multiple categories of martial arts styles and different traditions, as
exemplified by the concept of Bugei Jūhappan (武芸十八般, the Eighteen Martial
Arts).
"Bugei Jūhappan" refers to the eighteen categories of martial skills considered
essential for warriors, a concept that originated in ancient China. The specific
disciplines included in this classification are not fixed and may vary, but they
typically include the following:
Kyūjutsu (弓術) – archery
Bajutsu (馬術) – horsemanship
Sōjutsu(槍術) – spear techniques
Kenjutsu (剣術) – swordsmanship
Suijutsu (水術) – swimming
Battōjutsu / Iaijutsu (抜刀・居合) – sword drawing
Tantōjutsu(短刀術) – dagger techniques
Jittejutsu (十手術) – jitte (truncheon) techniques
Shurikenjutsu(手裏剣術) – throwing blades

By extension, Bugei Jūhappan has come to represent all martial arts disciplines as a
whole.
In Tenshinryu, while it may not encompass as many categories as the previously
mentioned Bugei Jūhappan, a wide range of techniques has been passed down in an
integrated and undivided form, preserved under a unified system.

Due to its inclusion of many teachings, Tenshinryu is difficult to fully organize and
classify, but its structure can still be understood. Fortunately, Tenshinryu has avoided
over-specialization and has been transmitted to the present day without significant
loss. When mastering multiple highly specialized styles, it is often the case that their
unique characteristics hinder each other or slow down the learning process. Although
not widely recognized, there is a real risk of functional incompatibility in such
parallel study. In contrast, comprehensive martial arts systems operate under a unified
standard, which allows practitioners to handle different weapons without conflict or
confusion and to apply techniques more flexibly.
In Tenshinryu, while the focus is on battojutsu (sword-drawing techniques) and
kenjutsu (swordsmanship), a wide range of weapon techniques and military strategies
are also included and systematically organized, resulting in a system of exceptional
versatility. It is believed that very few styles today have preserved

strategic teachings primarily transmitted orally*1 . In the modern world, nearly all

This preservation is fortunate, but at the same time, it presents the challenge that
mastering such an extensive system is not an easy task*2 . Aimless practice leads to
confusion and impedes progress. Therefore, teachings are transmitted step by step,
tailored to each individual's progression and capabilities.
Another notable characteristic of Tenshinryu is that it does not pursue perfection as its
goal. For past practitioners, the priority was how to apply Tenshinryu effectively in
their duties, not to produce perfect masters over the span of thirty or forty years. In
this sense, accelerated training was essential. Of course, it is natural for individuals
today to aim for perfection in their personal training. However, if instructors were to
wait for students to reach perfection before progressing, it would not be practical or
efficient.
While the professional applications of samurai-era training no longer apply to modern
life, the core philosophy remains unchanged.
