Yagyū Toshikatsu and Toshitoshi, and the subsequent Owari
Yagyū family
Yagyū Toshikatsu (柳生 厳勝), the eldest son of Sekishūsai and elder brother of
Munenori, is said to have been unable to inherit the family headship due to injuries
sustained during the Battle of Tatsuichi Castle (辰市城*1) in 1571 (Genki 2).
Toshikatsu's second son*2 , Yagyū Toshitoshi (柳生 利厳*3), devoted himself to
training under Sekishūsai, who had retired to focus exclusively on the study of
swordsmanship.
In 1603 (Keichō 8), at the age of 24, Toshitoshi was strongly urged by Katō
Kiyomasa (加藤 清正), lord of the Kumamoto Domain (present-day Kumamoto
Prefecture), to enter his service. He was granted a stipend of 500 koku, but he left

Tatsuichi Castle (Tatsuichi-jō) was a flatland-style Japanese castle located in Higashi-Kujō Town,
Nara City, Nara Prefecture.
*2 The eldest brother, Yagyū Sumiyoshi (柳生 純厳), was killed in battle at Ulsan, Korea, in 1597
(Keichō 2).
*3 Yagyū Toshitoshi was also known by the name Yagyū Hyōgonosuke (柳生 兵庫助).



the Katō household in less than a year*1 .
In 1604 (Keichō 9), he received full transmission and certification (kaiden inka 皆伝
印可) from Sekishūsai.
In 1615 (Genna 1), Toshitoshi began serving the Owari Domain (in present-day Aichi
Prefecture), one of the Gosanke(御三家, the three branch families of the
Tokugawa*2), as the swordsmanship instructor to Tokugawa Yoshinao (徳川 義直),
receiving a stipend of 500 koku.
In 1620 (Genna 6), he granted Yoshinao an official license in the Shinkage-ryū
tradition. Later, when he passed on the menkyo (license of transmission) to
Toshitoshi's third son*3 , Yagyū Toshikane (柳生 厳包*4 ), the license was formally
issued in Yoshinao's name.
From that point onward, within the Owari Yagyū lineage, licenses were alternately
granted between the domain lord and the Yagyū family, thereby solidifying the status
of the Owari Yagyū as an established branch*5 . Yagyū Toshikane developed
techniques suitable for beginners, such as Kōyōzei ( 高揚勢 ), also known as Toriage
Zukai ( 取り上げ使い ).

*2 Tokugawa Gosanke (徳川御三家) During the Edo period, the Tokugawa Gosanke ("Three
Honorable Families") were branch families of the Tokugawa clan granted status just below the main
shogunal house and allowed to use the Tokugawa surname. The three houses were:
・the Owari Tokugawa family (Owari-ke 尾張家 ),
・the Kii Tokugawa family (Kii-ke 紀伊家 ), and
・the Mito Tokugawa family (Mito-ke 水戸家 ).
They were regarded as potential successors to the shogunate.
*3 The eldest son, Yagyū Kiyotoshi ( 柳生 清厳 ), was a tatsujin ( 達人 , master swordsman), but
later fell ill and died in battle during the Shimabara Rebellion (Shimabara no Ran 島原の乱 ,
1637–1638). The second son, Yagyū Toshikata ( 柳生 利方 ), succeeded as the head of the family.
*4 Yagyū Toshikane was also known by the name Yagyū Renyasai (柳生 連也斎).
*5 The alternation was not strictly enforced; there were instances in which the license was passed
down for two consecutive generations within either the Tokugawa family or the Yagyū family.
However, it was never passed down within the same family for three successive generations until the
Meiji Restoration.



Nagaoka Fusashige (長岡 房成*1), who served as assistant to Yagyū Toshihisa(柳生
厳久), the ninth head of the Owari Yagyū family, created a set of techniques known
as Shiai Seihō (試合勢法*2).
The Owari Yagyū tradition has continued to be transmitted through various styles and
associations up to the present day, even after the Meiji Restoration.

*1 From the mid-Edo period onward, Japanese martial arts saw the rise of shinai (竹刀, bamboo
