About the Aoi no Gomon
At the time, the use of the Aoi no Gomon ( 葵の御紋 ) was strictly regulated. As a
symbol of authority, it was treated with the utmost respect and care. A kansatsu
(permit) bearing the Aoi no Gomon along with the shogun's kaō (handwritten crest)
carried tremendous authority. For this reason, precautions were taken to ensure that
the kansatsu could never be stolen or misused under any circumstances. It was made
thin and designed so that it could be easily broken and discarded if necessary.

Additionally, during special missions—such as delivering advance notice of a
domain's abolition—Kōgan samurai were permitted to wear haori (formal jackets)
bearing the Aoi no Gomon. Naturally, for both Kōgan and Tenshinryu, the two

Since ancient times, Tenshin sensei often spoke the name of Yagyū Munenori and
frequently used the Yagyū-gasa crest on various occasions. However, at the time, no
one had been informed of the true origins of Tenshinryu.
I repeatedly cautioned him, feeling it inappropriate—like a fox borrowing the
authority of a tiger—to emphasize Munenori rather than Tokizawa Yahei, the formal
founder of the style. Yet Tenshin sensei, unable at that time to disclose the true
background of Tenshinryu—that Yagyū Munenori was, in essence, its true origin—
remained silent.
Later, as Tenshin sensei came to clarify the historical background of the style in
greater detail, his reasons for focusing so strongly on Munenori and the Yagyū-gasa
became clear. As a result, Tenshinryu officially adopted the Aoi no Gomon and the
Yagyū-gasa as symbols of its deep connection to Kōgan.
The inclusion of these family crests on items such as the Tenshinryu flag symbolizes
the legacy carried by Kōgan and expresses the style's aspiration to carry that legacy
into the modern age.
It is also worth noting that even among Japanese people, it is not widely known that
the Tokugawa Shogunate's Aoi no Gomon underwent minor design changes across
different eras. In Tenshinryu, based on the period during which Kōgan was active, we
have chosen to use the version of the Aoi no Gomon that was in use during the early
Edo period, particularly up to the reign of the third Tokugawa shogun.
