Regarding the meaning of "Hyoho"
Tenshinryu was originally referred to simply as "Tenshinryu (天心流)."
Additionally, the cover of the instructional manual (Kyōdensho 教伝書) was labeled
"Tenshinryu Heijutsu (天心流兵術)."
However, Ishii Sensei instructed Tenshin Sensei not to refer to it merely as
Tenshinryu, but rather as Tenshinryu Hyoho*1 . As explained in the previous chapter,
Tenshinryu is a style that differs from others, and this name was meant to symbolize
that distinction. Furthermore, there were several styles during the Edo period that
used the same name, "Tenshinryu," so this change may also have been intended to
differentiate it from them.
The characters 兵法 are generally read as heihō, but in Tenshinryu, they are
pronounced Hyōhō. As seen in the well-known work The Art of War by Sun Tzu
(known in Japanese as Sonshi no Heiho 孫子の兵法 ), the term heiho traditionally
refers to the study of military science and strategy. It encompasses military theories,
tactics, and strategic thinking in general. However, from the Sengoku period to the
early Edo period, the term also came to be used as a synonym for martial arts,
especially referring to kenjutsu (swordsmanship).
Additionally, Hyoho (兵法) also carries the meaning of Heihō (平法).
In Japanese culture, there is a literary device called kakekotoba (掛詞), or pivot
wordplay, in which homophones are used to give words a layered or double meaning.
The character 平 (hei), meaning “ peaceful,” is the same character found in Heiwa (
平和), which means “ peace.”
In this sense, Heihō(平法) can be interpreted as the philosophy and methods of
resolving conflicts peacefully and saving lives. It also carries the meaning of
Katsujinken (活人剣)—the life-preserving sword—embodied by Yagyū Munenori.
For these reasons, we now use Tenshinryu Hyoho (天心流兵法) as the official name
of the style, in order to carry on the will and vision of Ishii Sensei.

*1 It is unclear whether the term Tenshinryu Hyoho—with Hyoho appended to Tenshinryu—was
actually used within the Kōgan organization during the early Edo period.
