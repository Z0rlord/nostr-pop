Founding and Naming of Tenshinryu
Tenshinryu is a martial tradition founded in the early Edo period, during the Kan'ei
era*1 .
Its founder (Ryūso 流祖), Tokizawa Yahei (時沢 弥平), studied Shinkage-ryū (新
陰流) under the renowned master Yagyū Sekishūsai (柳生 石舟斎). He later served
Sekishusai's son, Yagyū Munenori (柳生 宗矩), as a personal retainer known as
kinjū (近習*2), faithfully attending to him like a shadow.

Munenori later served Tokugawa Ieyasu (徳川 家康), the founding shōgun
(Shinkun,神君*1), and took on numerous covert responsibilities.
In the course of these duties, he organized a special cadre of warriors called the
Shirin-gumi (士林*2組*3).
This group later evolved into the Shirin-dan (士林団*4), and under Munenori's
leadership as Ōmemetsuke (大目付*5)—a senior inspector of the Tokugawa
government—it was formally recognized under the name Kōgan (光願).
As an elite body under the authority of the Tokugawa shogunate, the Kōgan silently
supported the still-unstable regime from behind the scenes.
Eventually, Munenori appointed Tokizawa to the role of close attendant, and ordered
him to organize a comprehensive martial system for the warriors of the next
generation who would carry the Kōgan legacy.
Drawing primarily from Shinkage-ryū and the Yagyū family's inherited techniques,
Tokizawa integrated the following elements:
(i) The teachings passed down from Yagyū Sekishūsai, Munenori's father
(ⅱ) Techniques and strategies devised personally by Munenori
(ⅲ) The innovations and methods of Munenori's third son, Yagyū Munefuyu ( 柳生
宗冬 )

(ⅳ) The tactical doctrines of the Shirin Dan*1 , the forerunner of the Kōgan
Tokizawa developed Tenshinryu as a ''Ryū (流 A term abbreviated from ryugi 流儀,
which means style.) that transcends Shinkage (新陰 Shinkageryu 新陰流).''*2
Initially, Tokizawa proposed the name of this new style as Tenshinryu (天臣流),
meaning ''the style of Ten (天)'s vassals (臣下 Shinka).'' However, this was rejected
because the purpose of the meaning of the style name was too obvious. The name
Tenshinryu (天心流) was decided upon as ''a style that follows the will of Ten(天).''
Ten (天) is a term with multiple meanings, the most representative being the sky.
However, in this context, it refers to the Tokugawa shogunate (Ryūei柳営) and the
Emperor (Tennō, 天皇) who conducts the Shōgun Senge (将軍宣下*3).
And Tenshin ( 天心 ) means Tenmei ( 天命 *4), Ten-i ( 天意 *5), Jyōi ( 上意 *6), etc.
Additionally, ''Tenshin( 天心 )'' is also a variant of ''Tenshin ( 転身 Refers to body
movement and footwork.)'', and this ''Tenshin ( 転身 )'' signifies the ultimate essence
(Gokui 極意 *7) of ''Marobashi ( 転 )'' transmitted from the Shinkage-ryu. In the
Tenshinryu, this is conveyed and emphasized as being "like a ball rolling on a
board." (「盤上転がす玉の如し」Banjyō Korogaru tama no gotoshi)
