Preface
This book is an introductory guide for beginners of Tenshinryu Hyoho. It is a
revised edition of the Japanese work originally published in 2012.
In Japan, there is a saying: tatami suiren(畳水練 ), which literally means “
swimming practice on tatami mats.” The expression implies that no matter how
diligently one practices swimming on dry land, one cannot truly learn without
actually entering the water. In the world of martial arts as well, theory without
practice has long been criticized. True progress is achieved only through diligent
training, marked by sweat and effort.
Indeed, the practical aspect of training is paramount. However, martial arts are
not merely physical disciplines—they are also paths of study and learning.
Without correct knowledge and wisdom, repeating techniques over and over can
lead not to improvement, but to deviation and even decline. To walk the path
properly, one must first understand what that path is and then follow it faithfully.
Training without a solid foundation of theory and understanding is like walking
an unmarked trail without a map. Unless one is exceptionally gifted, they are
unlikely to reach their destination—and more likely to stumble or lose their way
entirely.
This book compiles fundamental knowledge essential for learning Tenshinryu,
and serves as a guidepost to help practitioners avoid losing their way.
If only the extraordinarily talented or physically gifted could reach the goal, then
it would no longer be a true “ path.” It is called a path precisely because anyone
who walks it correctly can arrive. Tenshinryu is an open path—one that anyone
can reach, provided they follow it properly.
That said, Tenshinryu was originally taught only to a select few samurai.
Mastery of this path is by no means easy. However, in the modern era, its gates
have been opened to people of all ages and genders. Moreover, the path has been
structured in ways that allow anyone to proceed step by step. Not



everyone may reach the summit, but all can enjoy and savor the journey along
the way.
It is my sincere hope that this book serves as a lasting companion on that long
journey. Of course, what matters most in training is the direct instruction one
receives. By placing the highest value on the teachings received in the dōjō,
enriching your daily practice with ongoing study, and continuing to complete
this book through your own efforts, may we grow and walk this path together.
Year 7 of the Reiwa Era (2025)

Tenshinryu Hyoho, 10th Shike
Kuwami Masakumo
