Tenshinryu is transmitted to the 10th Shike
When I first joined Tenshinryu, only two students
were regularly attending practice. As a result, Tenshin
Sensei had clearly lost much of his motivation to
teach. Seeing this, I made every effort to invite
friends and acquaintances, doing my best to increase
the number of students.
Because Tenshin Sensei had a strong desire to have
more students, I soon suggested the idea of creating a
website and asked for his permission to do so.
However, he stubbornly refused. For nearly ten
months, I continued to persuade him. Eventually, I
managed to gain his reluctant approval to create the
website—though the reason for his initial refusal
became clear later on.
Around one year after I joined, Tenshin Sensei A photo from when I first joined officially
recognized me as a Daihan (代範, Assistant instructor*1), and the number of students
gradually began to grow.
I believe it was about one or two years after I joined Tenshinryu. One day, while
drinking together at an izakaya, Tenshin Sensei brought up a very uncomfortable
topic. He said, “ In truth, Tenshinryu is not supposed to be taught. This is because the
state has prohibited me from instructing it.”
Hearing this, I finally understood why he had been so stubbornly opposed to the
creation of a website in the beginning.
However, I soon realized that the directive he referred to carried no legal force, and I
informed him of this. To be certain, I also contacted the Mitaka Police Station to
confirm. They gave the same response, explaining that “ it was likely

meant as a verbal warning for a young troublemaker,” and that there was no legal

Thus, after nearly half a century of teaching in secrecy, Tenshin Sensei was finally
freed from the curse that had bound him for so long.
Around the same time, Tenshin Sensei also told me about Ishii Sensei's final words: “
Do not make anything about me public.” I responded, “ I believe that was meant to
keep things secret from the general public, not from trusted students.” From that point
on, Tenshin Sensei gradually began sharing stories about Ishii Sensei, and I came to
realize that much of what he had previously introduced with phrases like “ I heard this
from someone ” was actually based on Ishii Sensei's teachings.
As Tenshin Sensei opened up more about Ishii Sensei, he also began to explain the
origins of Tenshinryu in greater detail. Before this turning point, Tenshin Sensei had
said almost nothing about Ishii Sensei apart from his name. The amount of
information he had shared about him was shockingly little. On one occasion, while
drinking with Ide Sensei, he mentioned only one anecdote: “ The previous master
could put fish into a state of suspended animation.”
Ishii Sensei's name became known among the students only because Takei Sensei was
required to provide proof of lineage in order to participate in a public demonstration
(enbu-kai 演武会), and the name had to be disclosed as part of the application.
When I once asked Tenshin Sensei about Ishii Sensei, he stared at me intently, as if to
say, “ Why are you digging into this? Are you some kind of spy?” After a long
silence, he finally said, “ He was a quiet man.”
Such was the depth of Tenshin Sensei's loyalty to the promise he had made to Ishii
Sensei, faithfully upheld for many years.
In February 2011, I was summoned to the home of Tenshin Sensei. At the time, I was
extremely busy with work and initially declined. However, sensing his deep
seriousness, I responded to his second summons and made the journey to his home,
which took over two hours.



There, Tenshin Sensei handed me a sword engraved with a tanka(短歌*1)—a sword
he had shown me once before—and said, “ From now on, I entrust Tenshinryu to
you.” With these words, he privately appointed me as the successor to the next
generation.
「子も継がず、空しく枯れ逝く 九代で 流名御太刀に 刻み留めん」
"Komo tsugazu, munashiku kareyuku, kyūdai de, ryūmei ontachi ni, kizami todomen"
(“ With no child to succeed me, I—the ninth-generation head—will wither away in
vain. Thus, I will at least engrave the name of Tenshinryu on this sword, so it may
remain.”)

A sword engraved with a tanka composed by Tenshin Sensei. It has been appraised as the work of
Shinkei Tanemitsu(心慶胤光). It is currently owned by Ide Sensei.

This sword had been engraved in 1996 (Heisei 8) by a friend of Tenshin Sensei, a
skilled engraver (Horisi 彫師*2), after he had resolved to let the Tenshinryu lineage
come to an end. At the time of my appointment, I had been a student for less than
three years—an exceptionally short period for such a significant transmission.
Nevertheless, due to his advancing age, Tenshin Sensei chose to entrust the future of
the style to me.

*1 A tanka is a form of traditional Japanese poetry consisting of 31 syllables arranged in a 5-7-5-7-7
pattern.
*2 Horishi 彫師
A specialist who engraves patterns or characters onto the blade or tang (nakago) of a Japanese
sword is called a horishi (彫師).



Tenshin Sensei's friend, the horishi who engraved the sword, was a renowned craftsman named

Sakai Ikkansai Shigemasa (酒井一貫斎繁政). He and Tenshin Sensei were drinking companions,
and he would accept engraving requests from Tenshin Sensei in exchange for drinks. He passed
away in the same year he engraved this sword (1996).

However, just one year later, on March 11, 2011, Japan was struck by the Great East
Japan Earthquake. Tenshin Sensei had been planning a trip to Fukushima shortly
before the disaster. Had he followed through with that plan, he might have lost his
life. This near-miss further heightened his sense of urgency.
Additionally, following strong recommendations from instructors of other styles,
Tenshin Sensei formally granted me Sōden Inka (相伝印可) on February 11, 2012—
Japan's National Foundation Day.

At that time, I had not even been training for four years and was still very much a
student on the path of learning. However, if something were to happen to Tenshin
Sensei before he could confer the Sōden Inka, the legitimacy of the style's lineage
would be lost. For this reason, despite my inexperience, he entrusted me with the role
of Shike.
