# Source summary — Tenshinryu Foundations and Principles, Chapter Two (2026)

**Author:** [[people/kuwami-masakumo|Kuwami Masakumo]], 10th Shike (Masakumo Isshin Kuwami)
**Supervised by:** Tenshin Nakamura
**Published:** 2026 (Reiwa 8), Tenshinryu Hyoho Headquarters (Hyohojuku), Syōgetsuan 抄月庵. First edition, 139 pp., English.
**Provenance:** PDF from the user, 2026-11-20. Full text published at [[sources/kuwami-foundations-principles-vol2]]; this page remains the synthesized summary.

**Scope:** Foreign-language volume 2. Volume 1 covered history and philosophy; this volume covers **fundamental etiquette, basic movements, and basic knowledge** for beginners, drawn from the oral and written transmission Kuwami received from Tenshin Sensei.

## Key takeaways

1. **Terminology first.** Tenshinryu preserves early-Edo and school-unique terms that even Japanese speakers do not know: spatial terms (Ten-Shin-Chi, In-Yō, Mei-An), Nakazumi, Jinchūro, and hand shapes (Hirade, Ika-no-kuchi). See [[concepts/spatial-terminology]], [[concepts/hand-shapes]].
2. **Sitting and standing are techniques.** Eight seated postures (Ōgiza, Koza, Hanza, Kiza, Yagura, Orishiki, Sonkyo…) and formalized rising/sitting with hakama-sabaki; never place hands on the floor. See [[reiho/zaho-seated-postures]], [[reiho/kikyo-chakuza]].
3. **"Within the Nine Rites lies the secret principle" (九礼に極意あり).** Bows in shin/gyō/sō grades: 3 standing (ritsurei) and 10 seated (zarei) forms, plus gaze discipline. See [[reiho/bows]].
4. **The sword is never out of the equation indoors.** Okigatana placement (In/Yō/An), four indoor seated maai grades, sagetō carry forms, taitō/dattō. Never sit in ōgiza wearing both swords. See [[reiho/okigatana]], [[concepts/maai]], [[reiho/sageto]], [[reiho/taito-datto]].
5. **Drawing mechanics are life-or-death detail.** Koiguchi release methods (soto-giri, uchi-giri, hikae-giri), tenouchi (tatsu-no-kuchi), left-hand emphasis, koiguchi maintenance with kyōgi. See [[concepts/koiguchi]], [[concepts/tenouchi]].
6. **Training principles.** Sen timings (7 types), kiai as kiate (Yā/Tō, A/Un), jo-ha-kyū (sanbon de ippon), kaisho-gyōsho-sōsho and shu-ha-ri, metsuke systems, training-role names, kata-geiko (勢法) vs shiai-geiko, and why Tenshinryu says battō, not iai. See [[concepts/sen]], [[concepts/kiai-kiate]], [[concepts/jo-ha-kyu]], [[concepts/kaisho-gyosho-sosho]], [[concepts/metsuke]], [[concepts/training-roles]], [[concepts/kata-geiko]], [[concepts/batto-and-iai]].
7. **Ceremony anchors practice.** Yakujō (kinchō) opens and closes training; ken-zazen precedes it; the folding fan is standard kit. See [[reiho/yakujo-kincho]], [[reiho/ken-zazen]], [[reiho/folding-fan]].
