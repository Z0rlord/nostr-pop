On Ritsurei (Standing Bows)
Ritsurei 立礼 is a bowing
form chiefly used outdoors
and is divided into three
types: Shin 信 (真), Gyō
行, and Sō 草.
D o not bow by bending
the neck alone; bend from
the hips.
If you bow by dropping
from the head, the back
rounds, making the bow
unsightly and leaving you
u n ready t o respon d
promptly.

Examples of Incorrect Bowing

In the case of the Shin 信 (真) no Rei , as noted in the section on gaze, use
an unobtrusive upward glance so that it is not noticeable to the other party,
reading their cues and remaining ready to respond quickly in an emergency.
For the Gyō 行 and Sō 草 no Rei, do not use an upward glance. T his is
because the act would be noticed; furthermore, even with the gaze lowered
you can still keep the other party and the surroundings within your field of
view.

1. Shin
Within standing bows (ritsurei 立礼),
the Shin 信 (真) no Rei is the most
respectful form.
Incline the back deeply so that the
midpoints of both hands lower to about
the centers of the knees.
If you lower the back even further,
nearly to horizontal, it is regarded as a
special act of worship (hairei 拝礼)
within the Shin no Rei.
T his is used when worshiping deities or

T o express still greater respect in formal ceremony,

2, Gyō
T he Gyō 行 no Rei is the respectful bow.
Incline the back until both hands come to about
mid-thigh.

3, Sō
T he Sō 草 no Rei is the eshaku 会釈 (slight bow).
Incline the body lightly, with both hands resting on
the thighs just below the groin line.
In some cases it is performed with both hands in the
Ika-no-kuchi 烏賊の口 hand shape, lowered at the
sides.
It is an abbreviated bow; in T enshinryu this is also
called yū 揖*1.
