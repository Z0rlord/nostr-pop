On Gaze
As a rule in reihō 礼法 (etiquette), one averts one's gaze from the other
party.
Even when facing someone, it is a basic point of etiquette—especially toward
superiors—to lower one's eyes slightly to the level of the other's chest.
In prostration (heifuku 平伏), to detect danger in advance, one discreetly
uses an upward glance (uwamezukai) to read the other party and the
surrounding cues. T his must, of course, be done without letting the other
notice.
T his is a warrior's precept and an important teaching of T enshinryu.
It embodies the mindset of being ever on the battlefield (jōzai-senjō 常在戦
場) and teaches the coexistence of etiquette and the samurai's duty.
However, unless you first acquire correct etiquette free of discourtesy, trying
to keep the other person in view will only disturb your etiquette and result
in a lack of propriety.
Averting the eyes from the
other is also a sign of trust;
no matter how deeply you
bow, if you raise your chin
and glare at the other person,
it is discourteous.
T herefore, first establish the
correct form; after that, be
mindful of your eye control
( mekubari 目 配 り ) and
cultivate etiquette without
lapses. T his is essential.
