On Standing Up
1, Standing Up from Ōgi-za
When standing, you are more likely to plant your hands
than when sitting down, so take care. As a general rule,
when sitting down or standing up, avoid placing your
hands on the floor.
First, set both toes up and come to kiza 跪坐.
D o not rise with the insteps left on the floor.
In Shin-no-ma 真の間 (at a short distance), to avoid
closing the distance, rise straight up with both feet at
the same time.
T his is called chochin-dachi 提灯立, from the way a
paper lantern (chochin 提灯) extends and contracts.

Standing Up from the O kigatana



If, when standing up, you take a large step forward
with the front foot, or after standing advance the
rear foot to bring your feet together, you will end
up closing the distance with the person opposite
you.
T he principle of maintaining the proper distance is
emphasized not only in combat contexts but also in
etiquette.
When standing from okigatana 置刀 (with the
sword set down),
first pick up the sword and hold it at your hip, then
move into kiza.
After that, proceed the same way as the unarmed
method.

2, Standing Up from Koza
Even when standing up from Koza 胡坐, as with
Ōgi-za 扇坐, take care not to plant your hands as
you rise.
First, advance the front (upper) leg slightly, shift
your weight forward, and set the toes of the rear
foot upright to come into a half-kneeling position
(kata-hiza-dachi 片膝立ち).
Rise straight up, quietly.
D raw the front foot back to align with the rear foot.
If the sword has been set down (okigatana 置刀),
first take the sword, bring it to the hip as if
attaching it, then come to kiza 跪坐.
After that, proceed the same way as in the unarmed
method.
