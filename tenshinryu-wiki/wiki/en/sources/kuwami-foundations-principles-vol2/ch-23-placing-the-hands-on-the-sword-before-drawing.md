Placing the Hands on the Sword Before Drawing
T o draw the sword correctly, it is essential first to place the hands on it
correctly. T wo teaching methods help beginners learn this placement.

1. Gasshō-tsuka-kudashi 合掌柄下
T he first method is called gasshō-tsuka-kudashi 合掌柄下*1: lowering the
hands from a palms-together position to the sword hilt.

Place the hands on the sword as if bringing them together in prayer (Gasshō).

T he second method is called shogaku-gasshō 初学合掌*1.
Begin with both arms lowered. Bring the hands toward one another as
though joining the palms, placing the left hand on the scabbard and the
right hand on the hilt.

The Right Hand
T he way the hand holds the sword, together with how it functions, is called
tenouchi 手の内.
At the kaisho 楷書 stage, take care not to close the hand tightly around the
hilt when first taking hold of it.



Keep the four fingers together and lightly hold the hilt between the thumb
and index finger. Let the hand rest against the hilt as softly as possible.
As you draw, the hand changes into
tatsu-no-kuchi 龍の口, the “ dragon's mouth
” hand position, which is explained later.
O nce you have become accustomed to the
beginner form and can reliably avoid
gripping the hilt tightly, the tenouchi
changes.
Rather than keeping the four fingers
together, bend them as appropriate and let
each rest against the hilt. T he hand takes a
shape approaching tatsu-no-kuchi.
At a more advanced stage, when performing
in kyū 急, allow the tenouchi to form
naturally. T he hand maintains a consistent
degree of pressure around the hilt.

The Left Hand
Compared to the right hand, the left hand tends to be overlooked. However,
during the drawing of the sword, the role of the left hand can, in a sense, be
considered even more important than that of the right hand.
T his is because most people are right-handed and have difficulty controlling
their left hand with precision. Furthermore, when operating a sword, people
tend to focus their attention almost entirely on the right hand, both
consciously and subconsciously. As a result, the movements and functions of
the left hand are often neglected.
It is difficult to make progress unless you devote several times more
attention to mastering the movements of the left hand than those of the
right.
P lease study these explanations carefully and make sure to master the
techniques described.
The left hand uses its thumb to push against the tsuba and release the blade
from the koiguchi 鯉口*1. However, the thumb must always be kept away from
the position directly above the cutting edge.
