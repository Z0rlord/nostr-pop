Jo-Ha-Kyū — Three Phases of Pace
Jo-ha-kyū 序 破 急 is a concept
or igin at in g in gagaku 雅 楽 ,
Japanese classical court music, and
used in many traditional arts.
It divides the structure of a piece
into three parts. Its wider adoption
across the arts is said to have
followed its application to Noh 能
theatre by Zeami 世阿弥.

Although jo-ha-kyū encompasses various aspects of rhythm and speed,
T enshinryu primarily uses it to describe three levels of pace:
- Jo 序: slowly.
- Ha 破: at a moderate pace.
- Kyū 急: quickly.
It is extremely important to maintain a good balance between these three
levels in training.
Speed is important, but faster is not always better. What matters is using a
speed appropriate to the situation.
Likewise, simply performing techniques quickly during practice will not lead
to improvement if you cannot control and correct the movements that need
attention. It may instead reinforce bad habits and make you less skilful.
In Tenshinryu, three repetitions are regarded as one complete unit of practice.
(三本で一本 Sanbon de ippon)
P erform the first two repetitions slowly, then the third quickly. T he teaching
is that a complete passage through jo-ha-kyū constitutes one unit of training.
As explained above, practising only at speed will not lead to improvement.
