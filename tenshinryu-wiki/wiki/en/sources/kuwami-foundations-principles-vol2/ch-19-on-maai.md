On Maai 間合
Maai 間合 refers to the distance separating two people who face one another.
In Japan, importance has long been placed on this distance.
Maai is an essential concept not only in etiquette but also in martial arts. A
correct understanding of maai is therefore indispensable both for practicing
samurai etiquette and for deepening one's understanding of martial
techniques. T enshinryu places great importance on it.

1. Shin-no-ma 心の間
Shin-no-ma 心の間 (Kokoro-no-ma) is the distance at which two people sit
directly before one another at close quarters.
T here is no precisely prescribed measurement.
As a general guide, it is approximately the

between two people seated across a Goban 碁盤
(a board used to play Go, a traditional Japanese
strategy game).

2. Shin-no-ma 信・真の間
Shin-no-ma 信の間, also written 真の間, is another close distance. It
measures San Jaku(三尺 approximately 90 cm) to Yon shaku(四尺
approximately 120cm).
This is the basic distance for techniques performed while seated face-to-face.
T he name 信の間 reflects its frequent use between people who share a
particular relationship of trust. T he character 信 means “ trust.”
It is also called 真の間, taking its name from shin (真), gyō (行), and sō (草),
the three categories derived from calligraphy.

3. Gyō-no-ma 行の間
Gyō-no-ma 行の間 is approximately Go shaku han(五尺半 approximately
165cm).



This is the standard distance used when receiving someone in a samurai
household.
T he name derives from shin (真), gyō (行), and sō (草), the three categories
used in calligraphy.

4. Sō-no-ma 草の間 / Shisha-no-ma 使者の間
Sō-no-ma is a distance of Roku shaku (六尺 approximately 1.8 m) or greater.
Indoors, all distances of Roku shaku or more fall within this category.
It is also called Shisha-no-ma 使者の間, “ the envoy's (Shisha) distance,”
because it is used especially when formally addressing a person of high rank,
such as a high-ranking retainer or a daimyō.
It is used in circumstances involving a difference in social rank.
T he character 草 may be read as kusa or sō. The name derives from shin (真),
gyō (行), and sō (草), the three categories used in calligraphy.

Maai When Standing
T he distances used outdoors when facing another person while standing—a
situation called tachiai 立相, also written 立合 —are broadly classified as Shin
真（信）, Gyō 行, Sō 草, and Kaze-no-ma 風の間.
T he standing distances of Shin, Gyō, and Sō are not defined numerically as
strictly as the indoor seated distances.
T here is also Tsuka-no-ma 柄の間, which corresponds to Shin-no-ma 心の間
when standing, and an even closer distance called Mune-awase 胸合わせ.
T he standing distances of shin (真), gyō (行), and sō (草) are used
particularly in sword-drawing techniques.

1. Shin, Gyō, Sō, and Kaze-no-ma
T he standing distances of Shin, Gyō, and Sō differ from the seated indoor
distances bearing the same names.



Gyō-no-ma 行の間 is the distance from which a technique can be performed
by closing in with one large step. T his serves as the reference distance.
A distance closer than this is called Shin-no-ma 信（真）の間. A distance
farther away is called Sō-no-ma 草の間.
A far distance of Niken 二間 ( twelve shaku, approximately 3.6m) or
more—is called Kaze-no-ma 風の間.
T he name conveys the idea of a distance so great that the wind (Kaze 風)
can blow freely between the two people.

2. Mune-awase 胸合わせ
Mune-awase 胸合わせ refers to a distance at which the bodies are in close
contact.
T his does not normally occur, but it may arise when someone grabs the front
of your clothing at the chest, or when the distance is deliberately closed.
T he name means “ chest-to-chest,” indicating that the two people are close
enough for their chests (Mune 胸) to meet (Awase 合わせ).

Maai in Kenjutsu
In kenjutsu, formal practice begins at Kaze-no-ma 風の間: twelve to thirteen
shaku, approximately 3.6–3.9 m.
T his applies to nodate 野立て*1, or outdoor practice. When training indoors,
use approximately seven to nine shaku, approximately 2.1–2.7 m, as a guide.
Except in techniques that begin by rushing forward to attack from a
distance, both practitioners generally close in and begin their exchange at a
distance where the sword tips just touch or are about to touch.
T his distance is called kirima 截間, also written 斬間 and also read as
zanma.
Both 截 and 斬 mean "to cut."
T he moment or situation at this threshold is called zanma-sakai 截間境.

*1 Nodate 野立て is a term used in T enshinryu to refer to an outdoor setting.
