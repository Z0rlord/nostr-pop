Chakuza 着座
While the general act of sitting is called chakuza (着座 T aking a Seat), one
performs hakama sabaki (袴捌き handling the hakama) when doing so. T he
hem of the hakama often catches on the heels or is accidentally stepped on,
which can lead to stumbles or falls and, at critical moments, leave you a step
behind. Hakama sabaki is the movement that prevents such mishaps in
advance, and it is also important aesthetically, allowing you to tidy the
garment once seated.
T here are different methods of hakama sabaki depending on whether you
are not holding a sword or are carrying the sword in the hand (sagetō 提刀),
and each has several variants. As a rule, if both hands are free, use both
hands; if one hand is occupied, perform it one-handed.
P roper hakama sabaki is crucial for preventing blunders and avoidable lapses
when rising to stand or when sudden action is required. In all cases, be
mindful to keep your movements refined and beautiful.
As a general principle, when taking a seat, place your knees on the floor one
at a time.
A simplified form in which both knees are placed down simultaneously does
exist, but it is prone to creating openings in your movement and tends to
become rushed; therefore it is not used as a rule.
In methods that involve sweeping the hem, beginners may initially sweep
firmly to make the garment look neat. In actual practice, however, take care
to move quietly so as not to raise dust.
