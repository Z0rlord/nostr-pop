About the shape of the hand
In this chapter, we will introduce and explain various hand shapes frequently
used in T enshinryu, particularly those not directly related to specific
techniques.

Hirade 開手

Hirade (also written Hirate 開手*1)
refers to the open shape of the
hand and is used in a variety of
contexts, including posture and
movement, etiquette, and certain
techniques. It is a general term
also found in Shinto and is not
exclusive to T enshinryu.

Ika-no-Kuchi 烏賊の口
T his is a fist shape frequently used in
formal posture, movements, and various
forms of etiquette.
T he middle to little fingers are curled
inward in a stepped formation, the index
finger is hooked, and the thumb is
slightly bent to conceal the nail, forming
what is called Oyayubi-Kakushi（拇指隠
し, “ thumb hiding ”）.
It is used in situations such as standing
outdoors in a formal posture, or in
Sōkōrei（爪甲礼）, especially when seated
in Koza（胡坐, cross-legged position）.

It is also employed in contexts like gripping the Koiguchi（鯉口） during
sword sheathing or unsheathing, or when delivering atemi（当身, striking
techniques）, although the thumb's position may differ slightly in these
applications.
Ika-no-Kuchi（烏賊の口）, meaning "squid's mouth," is a unique T enshinryu
term derived from the resemblance between the groove formed by the
thumb and index finger and the shape of a squid's mouth.
