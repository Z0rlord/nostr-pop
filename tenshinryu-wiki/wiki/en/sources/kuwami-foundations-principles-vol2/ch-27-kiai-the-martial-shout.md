Kiai — The Martial Shout
Kiai 気合 is the vocal call widely used and valued in martial arts.
It can raise one's determination, increase concentration, and release
unconscious inhibitions, allowing the exceptional exertion associated with an
emergency.
Against an opponent, it can intimidate and weaken their resolve, or startle
them and create an opening.
D epending on the situation, it can also encourage allies or make one's
position known to them.
In T enshinryu, kiai is specifically referred to as kiate 気当て.
Ki 気 means "energy," and ate 当て means "to strike" or "to direct
something at a target." T hus, the term does not simply refer to vocalization,
but implies a clear intention to direct one's energy at an opponent, striking
them with it to provoke a reaction.
Kiai thus has an important role in actual combat. However, unless you
practise it fully and develop it through regular training, you will not be able
to produce an effective kiai when it is needed.
It is easy to feel embarrassed and hold back from using the voice fully in the
training hall. Yet it is that reluctance itself that you should be ashamed of.
Make a conscious effort to give a purposeful kiai during training, rather than
merely making a sound out of habit.
In T enshinryu, vocal kiai is used in kenjutsu 剣術 (swordsmanship).
As a general rule, it is not used in battōjutsu 抜刀術 (the art of drawing the
sword), except in certain roles, such as that of the kōtōsha 攻刀者, the
attacking participant.
An unvoiced kiai, like a forceful exhalation of “ Ha,” is used instead. T his
remains contained within the abdomen and does not involve deliberately
projecting the voice.



T his reflects the nature of battōjutsu as an immediate response to danger. When
performing nukiuchi 抜討, initiating an attack by drawing the sword, it is also
essential to act quietly. T he attacking participant may, however, use a vocal
kiai in some circumstances.
In kenjutsu, by contrast, a sharp, forceful kiai is actively used.
