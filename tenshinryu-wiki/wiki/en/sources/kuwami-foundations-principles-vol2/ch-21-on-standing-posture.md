On Standing Posture
T he position of the hands
wh e n st an d in g d iffer s
depending on whether you are
indoors or outdoors.
Indoors, keep the free hand in
hirade 開手, the open-hand
position, and rest it against
the thigh. T his is a formal,
respectful posture, expressing
deference to the person you
are facing.

If both hands are free, keep both
in hirade and rest them against
the thighs.
When such form ality is n ot
required, however, form the hands
into ika-no-kuchi 烏賊の口, the “
squid's mouth ” hand shape, and
let the arms hang naturally at your
sides.
O utdoors, keep the free hand in
ika-no-kuchi and let the arm hang
naturally at your side. If both
hands are free, use this hand
shape for both and let both arms
hang naturally.
It is also common to remove the folding fan from the waist and hold it in the
right hand. In some situations, the left hand may rest on the scabbard or hilt
of a sword worn at the waist.
