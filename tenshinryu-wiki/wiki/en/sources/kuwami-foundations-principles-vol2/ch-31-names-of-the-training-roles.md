Names of the Training Roles
T he following terms identify the roles used when practising with a partner.

Battō — Kōtōsha and Bōtōsha
T hese terms are used in sword-drawing practice.
T he participant responsible for attacking is called kōtōsha 攻刀者, or simply
kōtō 攻刀.
T he participant responsible for defence is called bōtōsha 防刀者, or simply
bōtō 防刀.
Kō (攻) means "attack," Bō (防) means "defense," Tō (刀) means "sword,"
and Sya (者) means "the person who performs an action."

Kenjutsu — Uchikata and Shikata
T hese terms are used in kenjutsu practice.
T he attacking participant is called uchikata 打方.
T he participant responsible for defence is called shikata 仕方.
However, shikata carries the sense of “ the person who performs the
technique.” Consequently, when the technique consists of an attack initiated
by the main practitioner, that practitioner is still called shikata.

Yawara-te — Gōte and Yawara-te
T hese terms are used in yawara-te 柔手 practice.
T he attacking role is called gōte 剛手, and the defending role is called
yawara-te.
As with shikata, yawara-te also identifies the main practitioner who performs
the technique. T he name therefore remains applicable when that practitioner
initiates an offensive technique.
