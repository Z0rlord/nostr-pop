Hakama-Sabaki in Ōgi-za (Unarmed)
Hakama handling performed in ōgi-za
when unarmed(mute 無手)*1 can be
divided into four types.
Because these applications have no
formal names, we use provisional terms
for convenience; however, there is no
need to memorize the names.

1, Maehiza-Suri 前膝擦り
(Stroke the Front Knee)
Slide your palms as if stroking from the
thighs down to the front of the knees
to handle the hakama.
First, with both hands, glide from the
tops of the thighs at the groin to just
below the knees, stroking the front of
the thighs and the hakama as you bend
both knees.

Maehiza-Suri 前膝擦り

Lower both hands into a V shape.

A general term meaning a hand holding nothing; also called sude (素手), toshu (徒手), or

As you lower your hands, do not
point the fingertips straight down.
Keep the hands in a V-shaped
arrangement, the reverse of the
Japanese character ハ, as illustrated.
Around the area just below both
knees, shift both hands outward to
either side while placing the left knee
on the floor.
At this moment, set the left knee not
straight ahead but slightly opened to
the left.
O pen the right knee so that it faces
roughly sideways.
Next, turn the right knee toward the
front as you place it on the floor,
assuming yagura 矢倉.
Here again, place the right knee
slightly opened to the right, as with
the left.
Move your hands from the knees
down the shins as if stroking, grasp
the hem of the hakama, and open it
to the left and right.
P lace the insteps of both feet on the
floor, lower your hips to assume
ōgi-za 扇坐, arrange the hem, and
compose your posture.

2, Hizaura-suri 膝裏擦り
(Back-of-the-Knee
Hakama Sweep)
T his is a method of hakama handling
(hakama sabaki) in which you stroke
from the back of the thigh to the back
of the knee.
Insert both hands, formed as tegatana
(手刀), from the seat side toward the

backs of both knees and lift the hakama hems.
At this time, work with the intention of creating a small space between the
backs of the knees and the hakama. If the hakama clings to the backs of the
knees, it will become taut and make sitting difficult.
Immediately move both hands to the front of the knees.
If you keep your hands positioned
behind the knees and then bend your
knees, you will end up pinching your
hands at the backs of the knees.
Set the left knee down to the floor not
straight ahead, but slightly opened to
the left.
O pen the right knee until it nearly
faces directly to the side.
Next, turn the right knee forward and
set it on the floor to assume yagura 矢
倉.
At this time, as with the left knee, set
the right knee down with a slight
opening to the right.
Grasp the outer ends of the left and
right hakama hems with both hands
and open them to the sides.
T hen place the insteps of both feet on
the floor, lower the hips to assume
ōgi-za, arrange the hakama hems, and
compose your posture.

3, Tsumami-age 摘み上げ
(Pinch-and-Lift Method)

T sumami-age 摘み上げ

T his is a method in which you pinch
and slightly lift the hakama.
First, at about the point where your
hands hang naturally at your sides,
pinch the hakama and raise it slightly.
With martial-arts hakama, this will
generally be around the lower area of
the side opening's end (aibiki-domari



相引き止まり, i.e., the bottom of the side slit Wakiaki 脇開き).
T hen sink your hips and set the left knee on the
floor, not straight ahead but slightly opened to the
left.
O pen the right knee until it nearly faces directly to
the side.
Next, turn the right knee forward and set it on the
floor to assume yagura 矢倉.
At this time, as with the left knee, set the right knee down with a slight
opening to the right.
Grasp the outer ends of the left and right
hakama hems with both hands and open
them to the sides.
T hen place the insteps of both feet on the
floor, lower the hips to assume ōgi-za 扇
坐 , arrange the hakama hems, and
compose your posture.

4, Matawari 股割り
( opening the hakama hem
between the thighs)
T his is a method for parting the hakama
hem by bringing both hands up from
beneath the groin as if lightly patting the
cloth.
Bend the knees and lean slightly forward,
letting both hands drop between the
thighs.
Lower the palms to about the level of the
outside of the knees, then flex the wrists
inward to sweep the hakama aside.
At the same time, place the left knee on
the floor not straight ahead but opened
slightly to the left.
