Regarding Archaic and Unique Terminology
In T enshinryu, many old Japanese terms are used as they are. Additionally,
unique terminology that was used within T enshin ‑ ryu during its early
Edo-period origins is also preserved in its original form.
While these older terms were common names at the time, they are no longer
familiar in modern Japanese.
In other words, even if someone has studied the Japanese language, they
would not be able to understand these terms based solely on contemporary
Japanese knowledge.
Naturally, the same applies to the unique terminology used exclusively
within T enshinryu.
For this reason, even for native Japanese speakers, training in T enshinryu is
not necessarily easy.
T o help improve the efficiency of practice, this chapter introduces and
explains representative terms and concepts that frequently appear during
training and should be learned by beginners.
In the chapters that follow, explanations of terminology will also be provided
as necessary.

Ten-Shin-Chi, In-Yō, and Mei-An
In T enshinryu, the spatial indications
upper–middle–lower,” “ right–left,” and
front–back ” each have traditional names.
・Top (Upper) - Ten 天
"T en" literally means heaven, sky, or upper.
・Middle (Center) - Shin 真
"Shin" literally means center or middle.
・Bottom (Lower) - Chi 地
"Chi" literally means earth or ground.

・Right - In 陰
"In" refers to shadowed or hidden places or objects.
・Left - Yō 陽
"Yō" refers to the sun or brightness.
・Front (Forward) - Mei 明
"Mei" refers to brightness, light, and things that are clear or distinct.
・Back (Behind) - An 暗
"An" refers to darkness, things that cannot be seen, or things that are
hidden.
Because these expressions are used frequently during practice, you must
learn them so that you can recognize and respond to them instantly.

Nakazumi
T he term Nakazumi (中墨) refers to the
central line that divides left and right.
It originates from traditional architecture,
where a carpenter marks the centerline of
a piece of wood using an ink pot
(sumitsubo 墨壺 ) and an inked string
(suminawa 墨縄).
Sumitsubo 墨壺
In Shintō (神道) and related traditions,
this line is often referred to as Seichū (正中).
Although the term is not commonly used in T enshinryu, many other martial
arts refer to it as the Seichūsen (正中線 central line).
