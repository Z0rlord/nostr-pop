Afterword

I intended to publish as simple a book as possible, but it became more voluminous
than I had imagined.
I have written only the minimum content, yet the fact that it still amounts to this
much information is, I think, a sign of how important the beginner's stage is.
Anyone who has trained to some extent should already have received explanations
and been taught almost all of this content. In reality, however, human beings let
much of it slip away.
According to one study, the amount of information that can be remembered cor‐
rectly from an oral explanation is about 47% on average.
In writing it is 58%, and with video about 67%. Since actual martial arts instruction
includes not only oral explanation but also demonstration, it may be said to be close
to video—yet even so, by this calculation 33% is lost (conditions and individual dif‐
ferences will be considerable, but as a rough guide).
I hope this book will lead to such a review, help you progress further, and serve as a
good guide for those who wish to study Tenshinryu seriously from now on.
These ten techniques come with supplementary knowledge and various applica‐
tions and variations, but if you first thoroughly acquire the content of this book,
mastering them will become easier as you grow more proficient.
Likewise, because the tactical, technical and motor principles are shared, it will
make mastering every technique easier.
One also sees the argument that basics are always the same repetition and therefore
meaningless.
Certainly, if one is repeating basics at the same level in the first year, the fifth, the
tenth and the twentieth, it is merely a waste of time.
But that is an example of receiving instruction of a very regrettable level. If one has
trained correctly, the quality will be completely different even with the same basic
content.

And however excellent the instruction, whether it is brought to life or wasted de‐
pends on the practitioner.
If you keep to the teachings, the technique will surely answer your efforts.
Do not make light of the basics; train with dedication.
Shimotsuki (November), Year 8 of the Reiwa Era (2026)
Tenshinryu Hyoho, 10th Shike
Kuwami Masakumo
