Goals of Training
For every technique introduced in this book, the following three items are first set
as goals.
1. Remember the name
Tenshinryu has a great many techniques, and it is difficult to memorize all of them
perfectly.
However, for smooth training and a deeper understanding of the Style and its tech‐
niques, it is desirable to memorize as much as possible.
Try, as far as possible, to connect the name of each technique with its meaning and
its form.
2. Remember the characteristics (Maai, Situation)
It goes without saying that remembering the sequence, form and movements de‐
scribed in the next item is important.
However, no matter how well you remember them, if you do not correctly under‐
stand and remember the premises—such as Maai (
, distance) and the Situation
—your movements will end up being wrong.

間合

Therefore, make sure you firmly understand and remember the preconditions of
each technique.
3. Remember the sequence, form and movements
Basically, this is the very purpose of training, yet surprisingly many people remem‐
ber it carelessly or remember it wrongly.
With the determination to read between the lines—as the Japanese saying goes,
"the light of one's eyes piercing to the back of the paper"—read the descriptions
correctly, understand them, and learn them with your body.
