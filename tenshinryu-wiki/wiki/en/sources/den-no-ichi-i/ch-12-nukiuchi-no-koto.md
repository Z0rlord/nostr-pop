Key Learning Points
— Acquire the basics of drawing the shōtō
— Learn Ken-Ki-Tai no Ichigan
— Learn the seated Tōnō-hō (Nōtō-hō)

Points of Caution
— When placing the hands on the sword
When placing the hands on the sword, take care not to open the armpits. Keep
both elbows against the left and right ribs and do not let them separate.
As a test, try doing it with a tenugui (hand towel) or the like held under both
armpits (upper arms). If the tenugui does not fall before both hands are on the
sword, there is no problem.
— About the draw
When drawing, take care that the tsuba passes along your Jinchū-ro (
centre line of the body) and does not stray to the left or right.

人中路, the

— About Saya-biki
Because the wakizashi is short and easy to draw, Saya-biki tends to be insufficient.
Saya-biki is not simply for drawing the sword; it is an essential movement for tak‐
ing the correct position of the shoulders and elbows and for balancing the whole
body. Do not think of it merely as a movement for drawing—pull the left fist and
the scabbard back sufficiently.
Also take care that the te no uchi of the left hand holds the koiguchi correctly.
— When taking the tsuka with the left hand
When taking the tsuka with the left hand, take care not to open the palm too much,
and do not let the left hand and left elbow open outward and trace a curve.
The left hand and left elbow should trace a straight line to take the tsuka.

— Open both knees evenly
You open both knees when cutting down; take care to open them evenly to the left
and right.
Do not open only one knee.
— Do not open the knees too far
You are told to open the knees as wide as you can, but seen from the front, if the
angle between the thighs exceeds 80–90 degrees,
they are opened too far.
The hips become too low and the hip joints are excessively open, so you cannot
move on to the next movement immediately.
— About the sword and the right arm
When you have cut down, as a guide, the sword and the right arm should form a
straight line. Take care that the wrist is neither under-turned nor over-turned.
— About Orishiki (Katahiza-dachi)
In Orishiki (Katahiza-dachi), take care not to lean the upper body forward. Also
take care not to tilt the body left or right; keep the upper body upright, with verti‐
cal as the guide.
The knees are opened to roughly a right angle.
The front knee opens slightly outward from straight ahead, and the rear knee is
slightly closed rather than straight out to the side.
Also, raise the rear knee as high as possible, to chest height.
— Zanshin in Orishiki
The kissaki points at the Nakazumi, aimed so that if the Taisha grabs at you in des‐
peration, you can step in with the right foot and make a one-handed thrust with
the right hand as it is.

— About Nōtō
After sheathing Kissaki Sanzun, continue sheathing while moving the scabbard
slightly faster than the sword.
— When finishing seated
After step 17 of the Movement Sequence, you may also finish by sitting down
(Chakuza) directly from Kiza.
In that case, from Kiza, put the insteps of both feet on the floor and come into Ogizahō.
Then, as when finishing standing, sheathe the sword completely, place both hands
in Hirate at the base of the thighs, then lower both hands to the inner thighs, and
finish by sitting in Seiza.
