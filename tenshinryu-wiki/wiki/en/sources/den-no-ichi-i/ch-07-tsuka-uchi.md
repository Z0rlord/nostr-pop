Points of Caution
— Always hold the tsuba down with the thumb

鞘走る

To prevent sayabashiri (
, the sword slipping out of the scabbard uninten‐
tionally), you must always hold the tsuba down with your thumb. When doing so,
take care not to place the thumb directly above the edge.
If the sword should slip out of the scabbard, there is a danger of cutting the pad of
the thumb.
— Do not lean forward
In Tenshinryu, leaning forward is important in many techniques, but in ordinary
Tsuka-uchi the upper body is kept almost vertical.
— Stand on tiptoe
When striking the Taisha with the tsuka, keep the front foot on tiptoe with the
heel well raised (Uki-ashi), and take care to keep the knee as high as possible.
— About the rear knee and shin
When striking the Taisha with the tsuka, always advance the rear knee until it is
close enough to touch the front foot. Take care not to leave it behind.
Also, the shin of the rear leg should point almost sideways. This is necessary to take
Hanmi.

— About Ken-Ki-Tai Ichigan
The timing differs between Kaisho (Shu) and Sōsho (Ri). In Kaisho (Shu), strike at
the moment the rear foot comes forward; in Sōsho (Ri), strike at the moment the
front foot steps in.
— Step back far
In reality, it is very difficult to step straight back without changing feet and take a
distance of six shaku (180 cm) or more.
However, by training to step back as far as possible, you acquire the footwork re‐
quired in Tenshinryu and strengthen your legs and hips.
Basically, the standard is that your shins must not be cut if the Taisha, in despera‐
tion, draws and sweeps with the wakizashi or daitō on the spot.
(Note that in this case, the Taisha is not assumed to move forward to attack.)
The reason for drawing back the front foot into Shumoku-ashi is that, by making
the centre of gravity a single point, you can turn in any direction and move a great
distance. Another reason is to guard against the most dangerous attack: a cut to the
shins.
— Do not step straight back
If you step straight back, it is difficult to step back far, so not only can you not take
sufficient distance, you also cannot get off the Taisha's line of attack.
Take care to step back slightly diagonally to the left.
At this time, take care to keep facing the Taisha.
— About Shumoku-ashi
Facing the Taisha, the kojiri should be just outside your right knee.

船漕ぎ

Also, be ready to perform Funakogi (
, "rowing a boat")—stepping the right
foot forward immediately and striking with the kojiri—if the Taisha attacks in
desperation.
Immediately take the Tsukagashira down with the left hand in Tatsu no Kuchi, and
operate the sword with the right hand to thrust or strike at the Taisha's face, etc.
