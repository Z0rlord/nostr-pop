Key Learning Points
— Acquire correct Gasshu-rei
— From Heifuku, a completely disadvantageous posture, learn a means of turning
the situation around
— Acquire the low posture that is characteristic of Tenshinryu
— Understand the use of the sword (scabbard) (the sword is not only for cutting)

Points of Caution
— About Gasshu-rei
Bow deeply without rounding the back as much as possible.
Do not raise the chin; look upward with the eyes to sense the presence of the
Taisha.
— About the right hand
When putting the right palm under the scabbard, take care not to place the thumb
on top of the scabbard or grip the scabbard.
— Keep the Tsukagashira on the floor
From the moment you stand the sword up until you strike the Taisha's wrist, take
care not to lift the Tsukagashira off the floor.
If possible, let it rub along the floor; if the floor needs to be protected, lift it very
slightly and slide it.
— Do not raise the head
From Heifuku until you strike and press down the wrist, keep the head as low as
possible.

However, since you need to see the Taisha's movement, raise the chin after stand‐
ing the sword up.
— Stand on tiptoe
When striking the Taisha with the sayajiri, keep the front foot on tiptoe with the
heel raised.
— About the rear knee and shin
When striking the Taisha with the sayajiri, always advance the rear knee until it is
close enough to touch the front foot. Take care not to leave it behind.
Also, the shin of the rear leg should point almost sideways.
— About Ken-Ki-Tai Ichigan
The timing differs between Kaisho (Shu) and Sōsho (Ri). In Kaisho (Shu), strike at
the moment the left knee comes forward; in Sōsho (Ri), strike at the moment the
front foot steps in.
* The remaining points of caution follow those of Tsuka-uchi: "Step back far" and "Do not step
straight back."
