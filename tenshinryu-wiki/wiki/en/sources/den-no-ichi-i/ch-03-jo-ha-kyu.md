About Jo, Ha and Kyū

序

Basically, training is first done at Jo ( ) (slow). Then, as you become familiar with
the movement, you train at Ha ( ) (medium) and Kyū ( ) (fast).

破

急
However, some movements—such as taking Seigan ( 青眼 , the middle guard),
Chiburui ( 血振い , shaking the blood off the blade) and Nōtō ( 納刀 , sheathing)—
are basically done at Kyū, except at the beginner stage when the movements are still
uncertain.

This is because repeating these particular movements at Jo does not lead to im‐
provement. When done slowly, some movements put a local load on the muscles,
or the sequence breaks down and the movement falls apart. Therefore, in such
cases, they are done at Kyū unless there is a very good reason not to.
Apart from such exceptions, the speed of Jo remains important regardless of your
level.
In Tenshinryu, even at an early stage, you learn Ha and Kyū as soon as you become
accustomed to the technique.
This is because there are things that cannot be acquired through Jo alone, and
above all, training at practical speed is indispensable for refining a technique to a
practical level.
At the same time, however advanced you may be, if you forget Jo in your training,
your progress will reach its limit.
