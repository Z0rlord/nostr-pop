The name Tsurube-otoshi (

The wooden bucket of a tsurube well is quite heavy, so it falls to the bottom of the
well at tremendous speed under its own weight. From this, "tsurube-otoshi" came
to be widely used as a term meaning to fall quickly or with force.
The best-known example is probably the proverb "Aki no yūhi wa tsurube-otoshi"
(the autumn sun sets like a falling well bucket), expressing how quickly the sun
sets. There are also waterfalls in various parts of Japan named "Tsurube-otoshi no
Taki," and in western Japan a yōkai (supernatural creature) that drops down from
above is called Tsurube-otoshi (or Tsurube-oroshi).
It is a technique for training, and its main purpose is to forge the body.

Points of Caution
— The buttocks when cutting down
In Dotan-giri, because you cut down strongly, cut with the image of cutting close to
the body, straight down, not far away. Therefore, when cutting down, pull the but‐
tocks back and lean forward.
— Cut down with the elbows extended
In actual Dotan-giri, the elbows are bent to cut down more strongly. In training
this technique, however, the elbows are extended.
This is to learn to use the power of the whole body rather than the power of the
arms.
— Pull both shoulders back
When cutting down, pull both shoulders back.
If you push both shoulders forward, the power is weakened accordingly, and you
will also hit the floor.
