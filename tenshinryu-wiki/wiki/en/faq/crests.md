# Why does the Tenshinryu flag carry the Tokugawa and Yagyū crests?

The flag carries two crests: the Aoi no Gomon (葵の御紋), the crest of the Tokugawa shogunate, and the Yagyū-gasa (柳生笠), the crest of the Yagyū clan. Together they stand for the legacy of the Kōgan, the covert corps of samurai that Tenshinryu was created to serve under Yagyū Munenori.

Kōgan samurai on missions carried a license tag, the kansatsu, marked with the Aoi no Gomon, the Yagyū-gasa, and the seals of Tokugawa Iemitsu and Yagyū Munenori. Tenshin Sensei spoke often of Munenori and used the Yagyū-gasa on many occasions. As he explained the school's history in more detail, the school officially adopted both crests as symbols of its deep connection to Kōgan. On the flag they express the aim of carrying that legacy into the modern age.

Tenshinryu uses the early-Edo version of the Aoi no Gomon, from the period up to the third shogun, because that is when Kōgan was active.
