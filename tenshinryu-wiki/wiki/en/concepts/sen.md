# Sen 先 — Timing and Initiative

Sen classifies timing in attack and defense. **Every kata assumes a particular sen**; practice without knowing which one is not merely ineffective but can make you worse (Kuwami 2026, "Sen — Timing and Initiative").

The cultural frame: a samurai lived **jōzai-senjō 常在戦場**, always as if on the battlefield, so a truly unprepared samurai was a contradiction. Even surprise attack (fui-uchi 不意打ち) had conventions, such as calling out before cutting from behind or waking a sleeping opponent; an unexpected attack was not necessarily cowardly, because a warrior was expected to be ready. Accordingly, classical traditions avoid the concept of **gote 後手**, being forced into a belated response: falling behind is one's own failure of preparation, so even answering a first attack is framed as **go-no-sen 後の先**, never gote.

## The types of sen in Tenshinryu

| Sen | Japanese | Meaning |
|-----|----------|---------|
| Sen-sen-no-sen | 先々の先 | Perceive the opponent's intent and the inception of the attack; act to control it **before** the attack physically begins |
| Sen-no-sen | 先の先 | Take the initiative: you initiate the attack. Also sen 先, ken-no-sen 懸の先, sakigake-no-sen 先懸の先 |
| Sen-no-go | 先の後 | Attack to **provoke** a reaction, then control the movement the opponent makes in response |
| Ki-no-sen | 気の先 | Seize the initiative through pressure, **kiate 気当て**: body positioning, the eyes, forceful presence, [[concepts/kiai-kiate|kiai]]; make the opponent hesitate or recoil |
| Tai-no-sen | 対の先 | Attack at almost the same moment as the opponent, like a counter. Also taitai-no-sen 体々の先; more common unarmed than in sword work |
| Go-no-sen | 後の先 | Deal with the opponent's initial attack, then counterattack; appears in many techniques |
| Go-no-go | 後の後 | Deal with a series of about three attacks, then counter as the opponent exhausts the sequence |
