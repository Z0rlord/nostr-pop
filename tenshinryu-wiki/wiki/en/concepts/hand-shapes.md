# Hand Shapes (Hirade, Ika-no-Kuchi, Oyayubi-Kakushi)

Hand shapes used across posture, etiquette and technique (Kuwami 2026, "About the shape of the hand").

## Hirade 開手

The **open hand**, a general term also found in Shintō, not unique to Tenshinryu.

- Align the four fingers; tuck the thumb gently inward so the **thumbnail is hidden**. This thumb position is called **Oyayubi-Kakushi 拇指隠し** ("thumb hiding"): conceal the nail, not the whole thumb.
- Cup the palm slightly, as if holding a little water to wash the face.
- Faults: fingers bent, or fingers spread apart.

Used in posture, movement, etiquette and certain techniques, including the hand position in [[reiho/zaho-seated-postures|Ōgiza]] and several bows in [[reiho/bows]].

## Ika-no-Kuchi 烏賊の口

"Squid's mouth", a **Tenshinryu-unique term**: the groove between thumb and index finger resembles the beak of a squid (karasu-tonbi).

- Middle to little fingers curl inward in a stepped formation; the index finger hooks; the thumb bends slightly in Oyayubi-Kakushi.
- Used in formal outdoor standing posture, in [[reiho/bows|Sōkōrei]] (especially from Koza), when gripping the koiguchi during [[concepts/koiguchi|drawing or sheathing]], and in atemi (当身) strikes, though the thumb position shifts slightly by application.

## Oyayubi-Kakushi-no-Kobushi 拇指隠しの拳

A fist with the **entire thumb folded inside**, used when sitting in Koza (胡坐). Its formal name is unknown.

- Said to express **"Koiguchi wo Kiranu" (鯉口を切らぬ)**, "not cutting the koiguchi": no intent to thumb the tsuba and loosen the blade for a quick draw (see [[concepts/koiguchi]]).
- Older readings treat it as hiding the "precious thumb"; the gesture likely has ethnological, ritual origins (compare the folk custom of hiding one's thumbs before a funeral procession).
