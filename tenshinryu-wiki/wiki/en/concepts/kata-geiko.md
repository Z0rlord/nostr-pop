# Kata-geiko and Shiai-geiko

## Why kata is the center

Fiction shows training as free sparring with bokken 木剣 or even live blades; in fact a bokken blow breaks bones or kills, blade-on-blade clashes ruin swords, and no one sparred with their everyday sword. Advanced practitioners can spar with control through **sundome 寸止め** (stopping just short), but the center of real training is **yakusoku-geiko 約束稽古**, practice with actions agreed in advance, learning the techniques and tactics preserved by the founder, successive shike 師家, and shihan 師範. That is kata-geiko (Kuwami 2026, "Kata — Structured Training").

## Why Tenshinryu writes kata 勢法

- Not 型 (a mould): the teaching is not to be trapped in a fixed mould.
- Not 形 (outward shape): training is not going through motions or imitating surfaces.
- **勢 (sei):** force, momentum, military strength. **法 (hō):** law, principle, in Buddhism also truth.

The characters express the school's essence: using **shin-gi-tai 心技体** (mind, technique, body) as the situation requires.

Strictly, Tenshinryu reserves "kata" (勢法, see [[concepts/seiho]]) for distinguished curriculum: the **twelve standing sword-drawing kata** ([[techniques/tachiai-12-kata]]), the **seven seated short-sword drawing kata**, and the **seven kumidachi 組太刀**. Everything else is **gihō 技法**, though "kata" is used loosely for convenience.

## Shiai-geiko — sparring in its place

Sparring with marudachi 丸太刀 (fukuro-shinai 袋竹刀) is old, in and beyond Tenshinryu. From mid-Edo, protective equipment and the shinai 竹刀 made sparring safer, and many schools drifted toward sparring-centered, sport-like training while kata practice thinned. The dismissal of kata as meaningless persists; Kuwami's answer: kata emptied of purpose is indeed dead, but kata forged in life-or-death encounters cannot be dismissed, and most critics have only ever met the emptied kind.

**Four conditions for shiai to build martial skill:**

1. Sufficient kata-geiko and high proficiency first.
2. Use training weapons as real-sword substitutes: only movements you could also make with a live blade.
3. No reckless charges or suicidal attacks that safety makes possible.
4. Approach shiai with a purpose beyond winning and losing.

Shiai 仕合 remains a substitute for combat, a form of training. It is distinct from **shiai 死合** (a genuine life-or-death fight), from **hatashiai 果たし合い** (duels settling a challenge), and from **taryū-jiai 他流仕合** (matches against other schools where a school's pride makes winning paramount). Recreational shiai has its pleasures too, but **shiai training must never become the end in itself**, a pitfall many schools have fallen into.

## From Tsuki no Sho 月之抄 (Yagyū Jūbei, on Munenori's teaching)

The problem was already recognized in Munenori's time; two passages quoted in the book:

- **On the proper mindset for shiai 仕相之心持之事:** limit exchanges to one or two blows. An old story: ink was brushed on shinai and the marked side lost, yet some refused to acknowledge being struck. A warning against prolonged, skill-flaunting exchanges.
- **On the proper mindset for training 稽古之心持之事:** do not overvalue shiai; follow the teachings with discipline. Only after mastering the initial, intermediate and advanced stages may one use shiai with the inexperienced as training; before that it is useless and brings no improvement.
