# Battō 抜刀 and Iai 居合

**Iai** is said to be the name Hayashizaki Jinsuke 林崎甚助 gave his sword-drawing art. Born in 1542 in what is now Yamagata, his father killed in an ambush, he devoted himself to training for revenge; in 1559, in retreat at Hayashizaki Myōjin Shrine, he attained his insight into the art, avenged his father, and taught his disciples. He is regarded as iaijutsu's founder, or its reviver; a monument at Renkeiji Temple in Kawagoe marks his final days (Kuwami 2026, "Battō and Iai").

The term later generalized, and schools outside his line adopted it, until iai and battō came to be treated as synonyms. **Tenshinryu does not use iai for its drawing art.**

- Early Shirin-dan 士林団 members included masters of various schools, among them accomplished practitioners of **Tamiya-ryu 田宮流**, which traces to Hayashizaki; they influenced Tenshinryu's drawing techniques.
- But Tenshinryu's drawing was built at its foundation on techniques transmitted in the **Yagyū clan 柳生家**, developing into a distinct system.
- The school says **bakken 抜剣, battō 抜刀, battōjutsu 抜刀術, or nuki 抜き**. (Tenshinryu sometimes uses 居相, a different reading-meaning of "iai", which is not about drawing at all.)

The reason is transmission identity: the name iai carried the character of the lines that used it, and adopting it casually for surface similarity risks eroding the school's distinctiveness. Things that look alike can differ in ways that decide a life-or-death encounter; Tenshinryu is careful even in terminology so the transmission reaches future generations intact.
