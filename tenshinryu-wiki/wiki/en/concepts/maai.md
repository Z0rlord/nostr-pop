# Maai 間合 — Distance and Interval

Maai is the distance separating two people who face one another. Japan has long weighted it socially as well as martially, and Tenshinryu places great importance on it: the seated techniques were developed for specific distances, and **training at the wrong maai leads to regression, not improvement** (Kuwami 2026, "On Maai").

## Seated indoors — four grades

| Distance | Japanese | Measure | Use |
|----------|----------|---------|-----|
| Shin-no-ma | 心の間 | across a single meal tray (ozen 御膳) or a Go board (goban 碁盤); no fixed number | hearts understood; confidential talk: "the distance of the heart" |
| Shin-no-ma | 信(真)の間 | 3-4 shaku (about 90-120 cm) | **the basic distance for seated face-to-face technique**; 信 "trust", from relationships of trust; also 真 from shin-gyō-sō |
| Gyō-no-ma | 行の間 | 5.5 shaku (about 165 cm) | standard distance for receiving someone in a samurai household |
| Sō-no-ma / Shisha-no-ma | 草の間 / 使者の間 | 6 shaku (about 1.8 m) or more | formally addressing high rank (retainers, a daimyō); "the envoy's distance", used across differences of rank |

## Standing (tachiai 立相)

The standing grades are less numerically fixed. Reference: **gyō-no-ma 行の間, the distance from which a technique lands by closing in with one large step**. Closer is shin-no-ma 信(真)の間, farther is sō-no-ma 草の間; they are used particularly in sword-drawing techniques.

- **Kaze-no-ma 風の間:** 2 ken (12 shaku, about 3.6 m) or more; far enough "for the wind to blow between".
- **Tsuka-no-ma 柄の間:** so close the sword hilts can touch; facing this way is **tsukama-dachi 柄間立**. With both swords in kannuki-zashi, it runs from hilts fully overlapping to pommels just touching; roughly the standing equivalent of seated shin-no-ma 心の間.
- **Mune-awase 胸合わせ:** "chest to chest", bodies in contact, as when seized by the clothing.

## In kenjutsu

- Formal practice begins at **kaze-no-ma: 12-13 shaku (about 3.6-3.9 m)** outdoors (nodate 野立て); indoors, about **7-9 shaku (2.1-2.7 m)**.
- Except for techniques that rush in from afar, both practitioners close until the sword tips just touch or nearly touch: **kirima 截間 (also 斬間, read zanma)**. The threshold moment is **zanma-sakai 截間境**.
- Tips crossed slightly farther: **issoku-ittō-no-maai 一足一刀の間合**, reach with a single step.
- One actual step from there: **shisei-no-hazama 死生の間**, "the interval between life and death", where a swing strikes.
