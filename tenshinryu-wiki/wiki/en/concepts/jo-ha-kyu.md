# Jo-Ha-Kyū 序破急

A concept from gagaku 雅楽 court music, spread across the traditional arts after Zeami 世阿弥 applied it to Noh 能: a piece's structure in three parts. Tenshinryu uses it chiefly for **three levels of pace** (Kuwami 2026, "Jo-Ha-Kyū"):

- **Jo 序:** slowly
- **Ha 破:** moderate
- **Kyū 急:** quickly

Balance matters more than speed. Always fast ingrains uncorrected errors; always slow loses sight of the purpose, since slow practice is a **means** toward real combat. The right speed is the one the situation calls for.

## Sanbon de ippon 三本で一本

**Three repetitions count as one complete unit of practice**: the first two slow, the third fast. A full pass through jo-ha-kyū is one unit of training.

## Jo-ha-kyū inside a single draw

In sword drawing the three phases also name the draw's anatomy:

- **Jo:** releasing the [[concepts/koiguchi|koiguchi]]
- **Ha:** sayabari 鞘去り, the blade leaving the scabbard
- **Kyū:** the blade cutting into the target

In combat these happen in a single beat; in [[concepts/kaisho-gyosho-sosho|kaisho]] practice they are separated so each can be refined.
