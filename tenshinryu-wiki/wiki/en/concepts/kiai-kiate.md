# Kiai 気合 and Kiate 気当て

Kiai raises determination and concentration and releases unconscious inhibition, enabling emergency exertion; against an opponent it can intimidate, weaken resolve, or startle an opening; it can also encourage allies or signal position (Kuwami 2026, "Kiai — The Martial Shout").

In Tenshinryu, kiai is specifically called **kiate 気当て**: ki "energy", ate "to strike, to direct at a target". Not mere vocalization but the intent to **direct energy at the opponent and strike them with it** to provoke a reaction. Kiai only works in combat if it is practiced fully; embarrassment at using the voice in the hall is itself the thing to be ashamed of.

## Use by art

- **Battōjutsu:** as a rule **unvoiced** (a contained "Ha" exhalation in the abdomen), because battō is an immediate response to danger and a drawing attack (nukiuchi 抜討) must be quiet. The attacking role ([[concepts/training-roles|kōtōsha]]) may use a voiced kiai in some cases.
- **Kenjutsu:** sharp, forceful voiced kiai. The uchikata 打方 calls **"Yā" ヤァ** (the arrow, ya 矢); the shikata 仕方 answers **"Tō" トゥ** (its strike, tō 当, against the shield).
- **Koshiki / Koden 古式:** uchikata uses **A 阿**, shikata uses **Un 吽**: the first and last sounds of a traditional twelve-letter Sanskrit set, together **A-Un 阿吽**, the beginning and end of the universe. The same pairing stands in temple-gate Kongō Rikishi (Niō 仁王) and shrine komainu 狛犬: Agyō's open mouth, Ungyō's closed. Compare aun-no-kokyū 阿吽の呼吸, perfectly coordinated unspoken understanding.
