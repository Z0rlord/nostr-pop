# History & lineage

## Origins (Edo)

Tenshinryu is a branch of Shinkage-ryū (新陰流) in the Edo Yagyū line. Tokizawa Yahei (時沢弥平) studied Shinkage-ryū under Yagyū Sekishūsai, then served Sekishūsai's son Yagyū Munenori as a personal retainer. In the Kan'ei era (1624 to 1644), on Munenori's order, he organized a complete martial system for the next generation of Kōgan warriors, drawing on Sekishūsai's teachings, Munenori's techniques, the methods of Munenori's son Munefuyu, and the tactical doctrine of the Shirin-dan. He named it Tenshinryu.

Kōgan (光願, "wishing for light") was the name given to the Shirin-dan (士林団), the samurai corps that supported the early Tokugawa government behind the scenes under Munenori, who was a senior inspector of the shogunate. Full account: [[history/yagyu-and-shinkage-ryu|Tenshinryu and the Yagyū Clan]]. Short version: [[faq/yagyu-shinkage-ryu|Is Tenshinryu connected to Yagyū Shinkage-ryū?]]

## Decline and preservation

As the long peace continued, the Kōgan shrank and was dissolved under the eighth shogun, Yoshimune. Part of Tenshinryu was taught openly at clan schools in Kansai, and that line is said to have ended. Kōgan samurai kept the rest in secret. The Ishii family carried it through the Meiji period and the war years in Kantō. In 1912 Ishii Seizō (石井清造) was entrusted with the formal succession.

## Modern succession

| Generation | Name |
|------------|------|
| Distant ancestor | Yagyū Munenori (柳生宗矩) |
| 1st (founder) | Tokizawa Yahei (時沢弥平) |
| 2nd to 5th | Kunitomi Chūzaemon, Yazaemon, Yagozaemon, Gorōzaemon |
| 6th to 8th | Ishii Tsuneemon, Ishii Benzō, Ishii Seizō |
| 9th | Nakamura Tenshin (中村天心) |
| 10th | Kuwami Masakumo (鍬海政雲) |
| 11th | Ide Ryusetsu (井手柳雪) |

Several names were handed down across multiple generations, so the true count may be higher. The list comes from Tenshin Sensei's recollection of the scrolls.

## 21st-century revival

Roughly fifteen years ago, Kuwami Shike promoted Tenshinryu widely, and the number of students grew from fewer than five to many dojo in Kantō. Ide Shike leads the international division, coordinating overseas keikokai and branches.
